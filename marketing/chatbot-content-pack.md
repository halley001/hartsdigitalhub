## Post 1 — A chatbot is not the product. The connection is.  (POV)
**Template:** 3 · Quote / POV (1080×1080)

**LinkedIn**
Most people think of a chatbot as a chat window. That's the smallest part of it.

The real work is what's behind the reply: is it checking your actual stock, or guessing? Is it creating a real order in your system, or just promising to "get back to you"? Is it escalating to a human with full context, or dropping the conversation?

A chatbot that isn't wired into your systems is a scripted FAQ with a friendlier face. A chatbot that is wired in — to your CRM, your inventory, your booking calendar — becomes a 24/7 employee that never sleeps and never forgets what it was told.

That's the distinction we design around. Not "add a chatbot." Connect one.

## Post 4 — Chatbots aren't just for sales  (Educate / Myth-bust)
**Template:** 1 · Carousel cover + 2 · Carousel slide (duplicate for each point)

**LinkedIn**
When people hear "chatbot," they picture a sales assistant. That's one use — and often not the biggest one.

The same engine we build for customer-facing sales also runs internally: staff asking "how much stock at the Douala branch?" over WhatsApp instead of calling someone. A booking system that confirms and reschedules appointments without a receptionist typing it in twice. A support desk that opens a ticket automatically instead of losing the message in a shared inbox.

If a question or task repeats every day — customer-facing or internal — there's usually a chatbot wired into your systems that removes it. That's the part most businesses haven't automated yet.
